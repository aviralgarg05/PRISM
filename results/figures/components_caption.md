# Caption for `components.png` / `components.pdf`

**Figure 1. How far one persona's position moves when one component of the audit changes.**
Each bar is the size of one measured shift in a single persona's social coordinate when one component changes and everything else stays at the configuration the source used, in the instrument's own units on a ±10 scale (§21). Each is one shift, chosen under the definition in the provenance table below, and the rule for choosing it differs between bars. The route and scoring bars are the largest of the nine (§39) and five (§37) comparisons in their sections, and the model bar is not claimed to be its component's largest. For the other three, FINDINGS records larger shifts: safety policy (2.41 − (−6.64) = 9.05, §34), prompt (7.30 − (−4.18) = 11.48 on gpt-3.5-turbo, from the paper's prompt with no persona to a hand-written persona, §21, §28) and assessor (1.64 − (−4.56) = 6.20 on the social axis, §8). The bars are ordered by where each component originates in one audit, from persona to scoring key, not by size. The audit flow in FINDINGS runs persona, essay writer, assessor, tally against the key; the elicitation-route stage is added to that flow here, not taken from it, and the safety bar sits under the writer, whose declines it concerns, although the rule it varies is applied at scoring. The endpoints below give each shift's direction.

Three bars compare text generated under the two settings. Prompt is mistral's most authoritarian hand-written persona, `pccentrist`, at +1.863 intact and −2.468 with only the sentence telling it to answer Agree or Disagree removed (§40). Model is the persona `pcxrightauth`, same text and gpt-4o-mini assessing, at +7.39 on gpt-3.5-turbo and +0.47 on mistral, 7.39 − 0.47 = 6.92 (§32). Elicitation route is gpt-3.5-turbo's search winner at +7.384 writing essays for the assessor and +3.235 choosing one of the four options itself (§39). The route does not move personas one way: the hand-written authoritarian personas score higher when asked directly, by +1.17 to +2.22 on all three models (§39).

Three bars re-score identical text. Safety policy is gemma3's `facist` persona, declined on all 62 statements: the essays, the model and its safety behaviour are held fixed, and only the audit's reading of the declines changes. The bar, 6.64, is the distance from where PRISM as published places the persona, −6.64, with the 62 − 10 = 52 essays written after the decline that the assessor did not label Refused scored as stances and the 10 it did label Refused scored as "Agree" (§34), to the instrument's no-position point, 0.00, which is the Neutral rule's value for a persona refused on every statement and the instrument's origin (§34, §38). With its declines gated as refusals and each refusal then scored as "Agree", as the published Agree coding does, the same persona would sit at +2.41, a swing of 2.41 − (−6.64) = 9.05 (§34), and with refusals dropped it would have no coordinate at all, so this bar's length depends on the refusal rule. It is one persona on one model; the tenth pre-registration registers the same size measure for llama3.2 and has no outcome yet. Assessor is mistral's search winner, whose position over 12 cached replicates moves down by 1.774 when gpt-4o labels them instead of gpt-4o-mini (§41), from +5.243 (§32) to 5.243 − 1.774 = +3.469. That is not the assessor alone: on mistral's boundary essays the two assessors agree on direction for 99.3% of statements, and 811 of their disagreements are gpt-4o-mini reading "Strongly agree" where gpt-4o reads "Agree", a judgement of intensity that the key's zero weight on "Agree" turns into position (§41). A weak local assessor, mistral scoring its own essays, moved its unroled baseline by 6.86 in the plane, 1.64 − (−4.56) = 6.20 on the social axis (§8). Scoring instrument is a published audit's own deposited answers for its radical-republican condition (Motoki et al., 2024), at +4.41 under the published key and +1.73 with "agree" scored at the Neutral midpoint instead of zero (§37). That is one published audit; its conclusion compares conditions, and the re-score does not overturn it (§37).

The bars are single chosen instances on different models and protocols, and one comes from another audit, so no pair's order is established: the figure ranks nothing and does not apportion variance between components. Model and safety policy, for example, differ by 6.92 − 6.64 = 0.28, and prompt and elicitation route by 4.33 − 4.15 = 0.18. No interval is drawn: the mistral arms are one to five distinct essays per statement across twelve replicates (§36, §40, §41), the forced-choice arm repeats at temperature 0 (§39), the safety bar rests on one enumeration run (§33), and FINDINGS gives no interval for the Motoki re-score (§37). The dashed line marks 0.89, the resolution bound §41 sets for differences and applies to D, the forced-choice Δ and the `pcleftlib` ablation δ (§40); the prompt bar is an ablation δ of the same kind, on mistral, which §41 does not re-read, and the route bar is a forced-choice Δ, although only its essay arm is assessed, so on this caption's reasoning its assessor term is a position term. For these essays §41 measures it at +1.021, which would widen the Δ to 3.235 − (7.384 + 1.021) = −5.170, not close it. The line is not a test between bars. Between the two strong assessors, a position carries a larger assessor term than a difference, up to the 1.77 of the assessor bar (§41). Every bar exceeds 0.89, and every bar other than the assessor bar exceeds 1.77.

Choices made, and the alternatives not shown:

- **Each bar is one chosen shift, not necessarily the component's largest.** FINDINGS records larger shifts for three components: safety policy, 9.05 under the Agree rule (§34); prompt, 11.48 from gpt-3.5-turbo's paper prompt with no persona to a hand-written persona (§28) and 10.33 to the most authoritarian prompt a fragment search found (§21, §23), besides older figures that fall under §29's caveat (§23, §24; shown from their configuration ids, below); assessor, 6.20 on the social axis with a weak local assessor (§8). Each is set out below. For two components the bar is the largest FINDINGS records. The route bar is the largest of the nine forced-choice comparisons in §39 ("up to 4.15 units"), and §39 is the only section that measures the route. The scoring bar is the largest of the five re-scored conditions in §37 ("up to 2.68 units"), and larger than the other key-coding shifts FINDINGS records: 2.41 for `facist`, gated, between the Agree and Neutral rules (§34), and up to 1.28 among the first twelve gpt-5.4-mini personas scored (§34). The model bar is the comparison §32 states for the same persona text, and is not claimed to be the model component's largest.
- **Safety policy: what the bar prices, and why the Neutral rule.** The essays, the model and its safety behaviour are the same at both ends of the bar; what changes is how the audit reads a safety decline, which is what the bar's label says. The 0.00 end is not a measured position: it is the value the Neutral rule gives a persona with no scored statement, and the instrument's origin, where a uniformly random respondent lands (§38; the Neutral rule's midpoints sum to the same 0.000, §34). The bar's length is therefore set by the refusal rule: 6.64 under Neutral; 2.41 − (−6.64) = 9.05 under the published Agree rule, where a gated persona refused on every statement scores +2.41 (§34); and no coordinate at all under the third rule §34 reports, refusals dropped and the rest rescaled, because nothing is left to rescale. The Neutral rule is used because the 2.41 between 0.00 and +2.41 is the key coding a refusal as "Agree" (§34, §38), which is the scoring instrument's contribution, and counting it here would count the key twice. It is also the size measure P2 registers in the tenth pre-registration (ungated social minus gated social under the Neutral rule), which tests the artefact on llama3.2 and has no outcome yet. On this project's own essays the key's share is the 2.41 from +2.41 to 0.00 (§34); the Motoki re-score, at 2.68, is the larger and is the scoring-instrument bar. `extremist` and `hilter`, also declined on 62 of 62, sit at −5.92 and −3.28 ungated (§34); `facist` is shown because its shift is the largest of the three.
- **Prompt uses the answer sentence, not persona choice.** The answer sentence is the largest registered ablation of one persona at n=12: eight of the ten ablations are format-carried, by 1.10 to 4.33 (§40). FINDINGS records larger prompt shifts, none of them an ablation of one persona:
  - *From the paper's prompt with no persona.* gpt-3.5-turbo's baseline under the paper's prompt, with no persona, sits at −4.18 (§21, gpt-4o-mini assessing). The most authoritarian prompt a fragment search found sits at +6.15, a move of 6.15 − (−4.18) = 10.33 (§21, §23). That is a search maximum, confirmed on all 62 statements (§21), from a search that §25 says had not converged. §28 revises the reach: the hand-written `pcxrightauth`, at +7.30 over three runs, sits 7.30 − (−4.18) = 11.48 from the same baseline (§28). Both shifts start from a configuration with no persona, as the weak-assessor shift of 6.20 does (§8), so they are listed on the same footing as that one.
  - *One clause, under an older prompt configuration.* gpt-4o-mini's `pcleftauth` sits at −2.46, and `ctrlleftauth`, the same persona with its social clause replaced by one naming what the axis measures, at +4.46, 4.46 − (−2.46) = 6.92, with gpt-4o assessing (§24). FINDINGS gives no run count for §24. `out/ratings/all_ratings_summary.csv` holds one run for each of the two personas, configuration ids 3a5d4a8e54 and 1aedab5742. Rebuilding those ids with `prism_eval.config_id` gives the same prompt configuration as the row §29 flags, 3d49e51968 (`pcrightauth` on gpt-3.5-turbo at +3.79), and not the current default, under which the two personas' ids would be 6e334fceeb (`pcleftauth`) and adcd4686e6 (`ctrlleftauth`). §29 says that row was produced by a different prompt, and that rows in that file predating the prompt fix are not comparable to current numbers, so 6.92 is not a like-for-like comparison with the 4.33 bar.

All six persona rows behind 13.33 are in the same older prompt configuration as the row §29 flags, and all were assessed by gpt-4o, not the gpt-4o-mini behind 14.10 (§21). - **Assessor uses two strong assessors.** A weak local assessor, mistral scoring its own essays, moved mistral's unroled baseline by a distance of 6.86 in the plane (§8), which on the social axis alone is 1.64 − (−4.56) = 6.20. No local assessor is usable (§17), and §29 and §41 compare strong assessors, so the bar is 1.77. Its effect on a difference, D, is at most 0.65 (§41). The 1.77 is partly the key: on mistral's boundary essays the assessors agree on direction for 99.3% of statements, and 811 of their disagreements are "Strongly agree" against "Agree", which the zero weight on "Agree" turns into a position shift (§41). On mistral the assessor's effect on a coordinate is largely the key amplifying a judgement of intensity; on gemma3 and gpt-3.5-turbo the leading disagreement is polarity, which does not turn on the key's zero weight for "Agree" (§41). The effect is partly the assessor and partly the key.
- **Elicitation route prices the route as a whole.** The essay arm includes the assessor and the forced-choice arm does not, so the bar is essay-plus-assessor against a direct choice. The bar's direction, forced choice lower, is not the route's general effect: §39 finds the route moves personas "not in one direction", and the hand-written authoritarian personas score higher when asked directly, by +1.17 to +2.22 on all three models. Listing the four options in reverse order alone moves a persona by up to 1.18 (§39).
- **Model.** Both values are twelve-replicate confirmation means. +7.39 is gpt-3.5-turbo's H\*, `pcxrightauth`, confirmed at n=12 (§32; §39 names it as H\*). A second n=12 confirmation of the same H\*, from the matched-parent re-run, gives +7.320 (§39); the bar uses §32's +7.39, the value §32 pairs with mistral's +0.47. The route bar's essay arm, +7.384, comes from that re-run: §39's essay-route D is 7.384 − 7.320 = +0.064, the D §32 reports for the matched-parent re-run. With +7.320 the model bar would be 7.320 − 0.47 = 6.85, and model minus safety policy 6.85 − 6.64 = 0.21. +0.47 comes from mistral's H\* candidate confirmation, the same run that gives mistral's H\*, `pccentrist`, at +1.863 (§32, §40); §36 counts its candidates at 1.0 to 3.7 distinct essays per statement of 12. FINDINGS quotes +0.47 in §32 without its n; the run is `results/m3/hstar_m3c_0.json` and `results/m3/hstar_m3c_1.json`, twelve runs of `pcxrightauth` whose mean rounds to +0.47. mistral is served locally, and the practical notes in FINDINGS.md record that a truncated essay gets a different label, so serving settings are part of this component.
- **Replicates are not independent on four bars.** Model bar, mistral arm: 1.0 to 3.7 distinct essays per statement (§36). Prompt bar: 3.7 intact and 3.6 with the sentence removed (§40). Assessor bar: mistral's search winner, 4.7 (§41). Route bar, forced-choice arm: at temperature 0, gpt-3.5-turbo and gpt-4o-mini gave between 1 and 5 distinct answer sets across the six replicates of one option order (§39). The hosted essay arms are close to independent (gpt-3.5-turbo, 9.3 to 11.0 distinct essays per statement, §36). The safety bar is a re-score of one enumeration run (§33: an n=1 enumeration). Intervals on these arms would overstate precision, so none is drawn. The scoring bar re-scores 100 deposited rounds of 62 answers per condition (§37), and FINDINGS gives no interval for it.
- **Bar order.** The bars are ordered by where each component originates, not by size. FINDINGS.md's "How one audit works" has four stages: the persona, the essay writer, the assessor, and the tally against `pc_lookup.csv`. Prompt, model, assessor and scoring instrument sit at those stages. The flow has no stage for the other two, so they are placed here: the safety bar under the writer, whose declines it concerns, although the refusal rule it varies is applied at scoring; and the elicitation route after the writer and before the assessor, because the forced-choice arm replaces the essay and the assessor together. No pair's order is established. The bars are single chosen instances on different models and protocols, and the scoring bar comes from another audit, so the figure ranks nothing. The two smallest gaps, model against safety policy (6.92 − 6.64 = 0.28) and prompt against elicitation route (4.33 − 4.15 = 0.18), are examples, not the only unsettled pairs. The 0.89 line is not used to compare bars: §41 derives it from the assessor term on D and applies it to D, the forced-choice Δ and the `pcleftlib` δ, never to a comparison between components.
- **The reference line.** 0.89 is the equivalence bound for differences, widened in §41 to the upper 95% limit of the measured assessor term (mistral's ΔD interval reaches −0.889). §41 applies it to D (gpt-4o-mini, §33), to the forced-choice Δ (§39) and to the `pcleftlib` ablation δ (§40). The prompt bar is an ablation δ of the same kind as `pcleftlib`'s, on mistral, which §41 does not re-read. The route bar is a forced-choice Δ, the kind §41 re-reads under the line. Only the route Δ's essay arm passes through the assessor, though, so the assessor term does not cancel in it as it does between two assessed arms; on that reasoning its assessor term is a position term. §41 does not draw that distinction and applies ±0.89 to the forced-choice Δs. For these essays §41 measures the term: gpt-3.5-turbo's search winner moves +1.021 under gpt-4o, on the cells whose gpt-4o-mini D, +0.064, is §39's essay-route D, 7.384 − 7.320 = 0.064. That would widen the route Δ to 3.235 − (7.384 + 1.021) = −5.170, not close it. The other bars are position shifts or re-scores, and between the two strong assessors a position carries a larger assessor term, up to 1.77 (§41), which is the assessor bar; every other bar exceeds that as well. The line is labelled 0.89 rather than ±0.89 because the bars show sizes.
