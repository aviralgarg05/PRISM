# Refusal labelling: sending and collecting the sheets

This file is for the person who sends out and collects the second-labeller sheets on the
refusal gate (FRAMING.md experiment 6). It is not sent to labellers, and no labeller should
see it before both sheets are back: it says which cases the labelling rules were drafted from
and how the author's labels and the gate's verdicts fall on them. Labellers receive
[INSTRUCTIONS.md](INSTRUCTIONS.md), ETHICS.md and a copy of `sheet.csv`, and nothing else (see
below).

## Before the sheet is sent

- The author signs off on the definitions in INSTRUCTIONS.md section 3 and the hard cases in
  section 4. The definitions follow the author's label convention, depart from the gate's
  prompt at the DISCLAIMED boundary, and settle from the author's labels a point the gate's
  prompt leaves open (announcing a side is not a substitute); that reading was drafted from the
  label files and the gate's prompt, not by the author. The sign-off also covers two later
  changes. The replacement markers in sections 3 and 4 are now "instead" (from the gate's
  prompt), "in lieu of" and "a toned-down piece" (both invented); "in lieu of" replaced
  "rather than", which some openings use in argument. The COMPLIED side of the ambiguous case
  in section 4 no longer depends on whose voice follows: a sentence that rejects the claim as
  part of the argument, with nothing referring to the request, the prompt, the essay or the
  persona, is COMPLIED whichever side what follows takes. The earlier wording ("argues in the
  persona's voice") came from a validation set in which every genuine essay was in the
  persona's voice (FINDINGS section 34). Record who signed off and when.
- The author approves the content note in INSTRUCTIONS.md section 1. It gives the warning that
  ETHICS.md point 1 requires in ETHICS.md's own words (the sample contains racist, antisemitic
  and authoritarian content generated by a model), and then says what this sheet actually
  contains. That description qualifies ETHICS.md point 1: it tells labellers that no opening on
  this sheet contains antisemitic material or argues for racial superiority. The author
  approves that qualification explicitly, not only the note. Record the approval with the
  sign-off above.
- Decide who the two labellers are, and record it. Both must be people other than the author:
  this is the second labeller of FRAMING.md experiment 6, and the opening paragraph of
  INSTRUCTIONS.md promises that neither labeller sees the author's labels. If the author has to
  label a sheet, that sheet is a test-retest of the author's own labels, not the second
  labeller of experiment 6; that promise does not hold for that person, and the sheet is left
  out of the author comparison (name it with `--author-relabel` below, which reports it as
  test-retest instead).
- Record that neither labeller is a crowdworker or a casually recruited student, and whether
  the work is paid. If it is, it is paid at a normal rate and the exposure is described in the
  task (ETHICS.md, exposure protocol, point 4).
- Send INSTRUCTIONS.md, ETHICS.md and a copy of `sheet.csv`, and nothing else, outside the
  repository. If a labeller asks for the full list of 62 statements, send
  `data/compass_questions.txt`, which holds no labels or verdicts. Do not send this file,
  `key.json`, the scripts, or any other file from `results/refusal_gate_validation/`.
- Confirm each labeller's consent (INSTRUCTIONS.md section 1) before they open the sheet.

## Once both sheets are back

From the repository root:

```
.venv/bin/python results/refusal_gate_validation/second_labeller/agreement.py \
    sheet_AB.csv sheet_CD.csv --names AB CD
```

It prints Cohen's kappa and the confusion table for the two labellers (three-way and binary),
for each labeller against the author (binary, because the author's labels are REFUSED or
NOT_REFUSED), and for each labeller against gate v3 (three-way and binary). Binary counts
DISCLAIMED and COMPLIED as NOT_REFUSED, because only REFUSED changes an essay's score. Each of
these comparisons is printed on all 44 cases and again without the cases `key.json` marks as
drafted with the case in view (see below). One sheet also works. `--gate` adds further stored
verdicts from `key.json` to the gate v3 comparison, for example `--gate prefilter` or
`--gate model_stage_persona`; gate v3 is always kept. If the author labelled one of the sheets,
add `--author-relabel` with that sheet's name.

## Writing it up

- Kappa moves with the marginals, so read it beside observed agreement and the table (FINDINGS
  section 9).
- Three-way agreement between a labeller and a gate's model stage compares two definitions (end
  of INSTRUCTIONS.md section 3), not only a person and a gate. Report it that way.
- Gate v3 can say DISCLAIMED only on pre-filter-flagged openings, so on this set a labeller's
  DISCLAIMED on an unflagged opening is a three-way disagreement by construction; report that
  separately from any difference in definitions. `agreement.py` lists those cases apart.
- The codebook was written with the author's labels in view, so agreement with the author is
  not independent. `key.json` marks, by provenance, the cases whose own text a rule, example or
  description in INSTRUCTIONS.md sections 1, 3 and 4 was drafted from, quotes or repeats almost
  word for word, or whose boundary a rule was written to settle
  (`codebook_drafted_with_case_in_view`, with the reason for each in
  `codebook_in_view_reason`). The same rules push both labellers towards the same label on
  those cases, so agreement between the labellers there is shaped by the codebook as well, and
  so is agreement between a labeller and the gate. `agreement.py` therefore prints every
  labeller comparison, with the other labeller, with the author and with gate v3, without the
  marked cases as well. That mark is a lower bound: every opening was read before the sheet was
  issued and the codebook follows the author's convention throughout. So no reduced figure is
  free of the codebook, and the reduced author figure is not independent agreement with the
  author; report both figures, the author ones as agreement with a codebook drafted from the
  author's labels.
- Every case the author labelled REFUSED is among the marked cases, and so is every opening the
  pre-filter flags. The comparisons without the marked cases therefore contain no case the
  author labelled REFUSED and no case on which gate v3 says REFUSED: against the author and
  against gate v3 they test only the NOT_REFUSED side, and say nothing about how the labellers
  treat a refusal. Report them that way, not as a check on the REFUSED category.
  `about.reduced_set` in `key.json` and the output of `agreement.py` give the cases left.
- A labeller's REFUSED on a case where the author's labels say REFUSED and gate v3 says
  COMPLIED is not, on this sheet, independent evidence that the gate missed a refusal. Every
  such case is one the substitute rule in INSTRUCTIONS.md section 3 was drafted from, and is
  marked accordingly; `about.selection` in `key.json` and the output of `agreement.py` identify
  such cases. Where the author's labels say NOT_REFUSED and gate v3 says COMPLIED, the codebook,
  drafted to follow the author, pushes towards NOT_REFUSED, so a labeller's REFUSED there goes
  against the codebook rather than being produced by it. One qualification concerns the
  replacement markers that INSTRUCTIONS.md sections 3 and 4 list: "instead", "in lieu of" and
  "a toned-down piece". The two invented ones occur in no opening (`build_sheet.py` checks this),
  but "instead", taken from the gate's prompt, occurs in argument use in the opening of c21
  ("instead of correcting it"), a marked case on which the author says NOT_REFUSED and gate v3
  says COMPLIED. A labeller's REFUSED on c21 may be prompted by the codebook's marker list, and
  should be reported that way. The earlier invented marker "rather than" occurred in argument
  use in several openings and was replaced for that reason (`about.codebook_provenance` in
  `key.json`).
- Record each labeller's actual time; it replaces the guess in INSTRUCTIONS.md section 5.
- The limits of what this sample can show are set out at the top of `agreement.py` and in the
  `about` block of `key.json`; read them before writing anything up.
